## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment — authored for this product, on a borrowed identity.**

The *composition* is specific and hard to fake. The `.eff` readout beside every percentage ("15 → +38 950 kr") is a real invention: it turns an abstract knob into money without a second screen. Hiding unpriced roles and explaining the hiding, per-currency `Intl` locales so kronor come out whole and euros keep cents, client-facing rows rounded per line so the document adds up by hand, the drawn verdict badge family at a deliberately different scale from the control icons — none of that is category-interchangeable. No generic pricing calculator would arrive here.

The *visual world* is not authored. It is iOS, closely: the 51×31 green toggle, `#0071E3` / `#0A84FF`, SF Pro, the glass header with `saturate(180%)`, `cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1)`. It is executed better than most web imitations of it. But PRODUCT.md already says the treatment is "an unbriefed default, not an identity the user has committed to", and that is exactly what it reads as — a very well-made Settings.app for pricing. For an Operate surface that is a defensible default, not a failure. It just means the tool has no visual position of its own, and the Swedish-consultancy specificity that is the product's moat is nowhere in the look.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs` returned one advisory finding on `index.html`: em-dash saturation, 9 in body text. Fair. The em-dash is doing every connective job in the microcopy — "Nothing to price yet — add a line below", "3 unpriced roles hidden — give them rates", "No live rates yet — enter them manually", "Setup — step 1 of 3", "62,1 % — above your 35 % target". Vary two or three to colons and the voice stops sounding like one cadence on repeat.

My own composited contrast pass (walking the full alpha stack, both themes, four verdict states) found one real AA failure that the detector does not cover, and no others: see P4.

**Visual overlays.** Not attempted — no browser-automation tool is exposed in this session. I rendered evidence instead: 11 states across desktop / 861 px / 390 px / 320 px, light and dark, captured through a scripted Chromium with seeded realistic data.

## Overall Impression

This is the most carefully made thing I have reviewed in a while, and it has a hole straight through the middle of it.

The surface craft is real: the tabular figures, the size-specific tracking, the money-effect readouts, the way the verdict badge is drawn at badge scale so it never reads as pressable. Someone thought about vibrancy on small text over glass. That level of attention is rare.

And then the margin verdict — the thing PRODUCT.md calls "the reason to open this instead of a spreadsheet" — renders a green checkmark and "above your 35 % target" on a quote that loses 121 808 kr. It is also the last element on the page, roughly two viewport-heights below the price on mobile. The product's stated first principle is that the verdict outranks the arithmetic. The layout, the type scale and the failure behaviour all say the opposite.

The single biggest opportunity: make the verdict the load-bearing element it claims to be — correct at the edges, first in the reading order, and loud enough that a bad one cannot be mistaken for a good one.

## What's Working

**The `.eff` money-effect readouts.** "Risk buffer % [15] → +38 950 kr". Every abstract percentage states its consequence in kronor, live, inline, in dimmed 13 px so it never competes with the input. This is the best idea in the interface and it is applied consistently across risk, discount, retainer discount, startup fee and moms. Most tools make you hunt the total for the delta.

**Numeric typography.** `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` globally, `Intl.NumberFormat` per currency with `sv-SE` grouping so it reads 283 683 kr not 283,683, decimals suppressed for SEK and kept for EUR/USD/GBP, tracking tightened at 30 px display size (`-.026em`) and opened at 12.5 px. Columns align down the breakdown without a single manual nudge. This is the part a spreadsheet actually loses to.

**Restraint under pressure.** A dense financial tool with three modes, two directions, four currencies and a settings surface, and there is no decoration anywhere — no gradient, no illustration, no card that exists to fill space. 320 px produces zero horizontal overflow. The reduced-motion and reduced-transparency queries are both handled, and handled thoughtfully rather than by blanket-disabling.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The margin verdict lies on negative revenue

Set discount to 500 % (nothing stops you). Total ex moms becomes **−121 808 kr**. The verdict renders **green, with a check icon: "105,3 % — above your 35 % target"**.

The arithmetic is `profit / totalExVat * 100`; both go negative, the signs cancel, and the healthiest-looking badge in the app sits on the worst quote in it. Nothing bounds any percentage input — discount, risk, retainer discount, admin, target margin and moms are all unbounded in both directions.

**Why it matters:** this is the one number the user is meant to trust enough to say no on. A verdict that is confidently, colourfully wrong at the edge is worse than no verdict, because the user has been taught to rely on it. It is also the exact failure a margin floor exists to prevent.

**Fix:** guard the verdict before you guard the input. Treat `totalExVat <= 0` as its own state — a `bad` badge reading "No revenue — this quote costs you money" — rather than letting it fall through the percentage branch. Then clamp: `max="100"` on discount and retainer discount, `max="100"` on target margin, sane maxima on risk and admin, and reject the out-of-range value at `num()` rather than storing it. One assertion in the existing self-check block covers the regression: a 500 % discount must not produce a `good` class.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The verdict is last, and quietest, on the surface it exists to govern

On mobile the price lands at y≈1343 and the verdict at y≈1590 in an 844 px viewport — you scroll roughly two screens past the answer to reach the judgement. On desktop it sits below both copy buttons, under a second `h2`, at the bottom of the right column.

Worse, the states are near-equal in weight. "62,1 % — above target" and "20 % — below target" are the same 15 px, same padding, same 7 % tint, differing only in hue and glyph. The 30 px `283 683 kr` above them is four times louder than either. A price you should refuse is displayed more prominently than the refusal.

**Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md principle 1 says the verdict "should never be something the user has to go looking for". Right now it is the thing you scroll to last. And a warning that whispers at the same volume as an all-clear is not a warning.

**Fix:** move the verdict above the Breakdown table, directly under the `Breakdown` heading — it reads as a status line on the panel, not a footnote to it. Give the non-good states real weight: heavier tint, a left rule in the state colour, and the failing input named in the badge ("Discount 55 % is what pushed you under"). Keep the good state exactly as quiet as it is now; the asymmetry is the point.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P1] Budget mode asks a question the answer panel ignores

Switch **Start from** to "Budget → hours", enter 250 000 kr. The right panel still reads **Total ex moms 283 683 kr** — the forward calculation, contradicting the budget by 34 000 kr with no acknowledgement. The actual answer lives in the left column as a 13 px `--dim` run-on sentence: "250 000 kr ex moms ≈ 147,4 h at 1 338 kr/h blended · Strategy 21,1 h, Design 75,5 h, Production 35,1 h, Art direction 15,8 h".

**Why it matters:** the user changed the tool's direction and the tool's answer panel did not turn around. The most important derived numbers in the mode — the hours the budget buys, split per role — are rendered in the least legible thing on screen, a grey hint paragraph, while a contradicting total keeps the display type. A user skimming for the answer reads the wrong number.

**Fix:** when `direction === "budget"`, the Breakdown panel's headline becomes the solved hours, and the per-role split becomes real table rows with the same tabular alignment as everything else. Show the client's budget as the anchor and the forward total only as a reconciliation line if it differs. One direction, one answer, one place.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

### [P2] Nothing is announced, and nothing is confirmed

`document.querySelectorAll('[aria-live],[role=status],[role=alert]').length === 0`. Every keystroke in an hours field rewrites the breakdown, the total, the verdict and five effect readouts. A screen-reader user hears none of it — they type into a void and would have to navigate the whole right column to discover what changed.

The same gap has a sighted form: saving a quote produces no confirmation, only a quiet header-label change from italic "Unsaved quote" to the name. Copy failure shows "Copy failed" for 1.3 s with no reason and no retry.

**Why it matters:** for a tool whose entire output is a recomputed number, "the number changed" is the only feedback that matters, and it is the one thing never announced. This is not an edge case for Sam — it is the primary loop.

**Fix:** `aria-live="polite"` on a small status node carrying total + verdict, updated on a short debounce so it announces the settled value rather than every intermediate keystroke. Give `#margin` `role="status"`. Replace the three `prompt()` / two `alert()` calls with in-system UI — a named-quote row and an inline error line — so confirmation and failure live where the eye already is.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] Destructive by one click, recoverable by none

Clicking the × in the rate card deletes the rate **and** silently strips it from the open quote. Measured: 1 rate → 0 rates, no dialog. There is no undo anywhere in the app, and the × is a 38 px unlabelled icon-only button — the smallest target in the row and the only destructive one.

Separately, "Copy for client" is a bright blue primary CTA on an empty quote and produces a real, pasteable document: `Offert / Uppstart 9 200 kr / Summa exkl. moms 9 200 kr / Moms 25 % 2 300 kr / Summa inkl. moms 11 500 kr`. A quote containing no work, priced and ready to email.

**Why it matters:** the clipboard is the product's output. A primary button that cheerfully emits a bogus client document is the highest-consequence error path in the app, and it is styled as the recommended action. The rate-card delete is the same failure in the other direction — maximum consequence, minimum friction.

**Fix:** confirm the rate-card delete when the rate is in use, naming what else it will change ("Design is used in this quote — remove it from both?"). Disable both copy buttons when `c.hours === 0`, with the reason on the button's `title`. Prefer an undo toast over a confirm dialog wherever you can afford one — it costs the user nothing on the 99 % of deletions that were intentional.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Sam (accessibility-dependent).** Zero live regions — types 86 into Design, hears silence, has no idea the total moved. `#margin` is a plain `div`, so the verdict is not announced when it flips from green to amber. Every interactive control on mobile is under the 44 pt target: the remove × is 38×38, the Save/Duplicate/Delete row is 31 px tall, "Export backup" is a 15 px-tall link. Focus indicators are present and correct (`:focus-visible`, 2 px accent, offset tuned per control type) and the tab order is genuinely clean — viewToggle → mode → currency → direction → hours/remove pairs in visual order. Contrast passes AA everywhere in light mode; dark mode fails on exactly one element (P4). Good bones, no announcements.

**Alex (power user).** Quotes all day, wants ⌘S — gets `prompt("Quote name")`, a native modal that breaks the flow and cannot be styled or escaped consistently. No Esc to leave Settings, no Enter to save, no keyboard path to duplicate. Cannot see the rate card and the quote at once, so tuning a rate against a live margin means bouncing views and losing the total from sight each time. Notices immediately that the saved-quotes picker truncates to "Nordwell — brand system ·" and can't tell two versions of the same client apart.

**Riley (stress tester).** Finds the 500 % discount in under a minute and gets the green "105,3 % — above target" badge — then stops trusting the tool entirely. Sets hours to 0 and successfully copies an 11 500 kr client quote for no work. Deletes a rate mid-quote and watches a line vanish from the breakdown with no warning and no way back. Types a discount of 200 and gets a negative total displayed at 30 px in the same confident weight as a real one.

## Minor Observations

- **Three control heights in one row.** Measured in the rate card: `input` 38.5 px, `button` 38 px, `select` 36 px, bottom-aligned — so their tops stagger at 160.7 / 161.2 / 163.2. The SEK dropdown sits 2.5 px low against the Rate/h field beside it, visibly, on every row. One `min-height: 38px` across `input, select, button` fixes the whole column.
- **Retainer mode emphasises the wrong figure.** "Month 1 (incl. startup) 223 173 kr" gets the 30 px display type; "Months 2+ 215 173 kr" and "Contract value · 6 mo 1 299 036 kr" get 15 px regular. Month 1 is an artifact of the startup fee and differs from the recurring figure by 3 %. The two numbers a retainer is actually negotiated on are the monthly and the contract value.
- **"Break-even 443,3 h (you estimated 168 h)"** reads as "you are 275 hours short" when it means the opposite — you have 275 hours of headroom. Either invert it to "Headroom 275,3 h beyond your estimate" or label it "Hours before this quote breaks even".
- **"Target 35 % needs ≥ 985 kr"** is missing its unit. It's a rate per hour; say so.
- **Settings view leaves a large void.** The right column ends roughly 400 px above the left on a 1280 canvas. Presets and Backup could move under Fees, or the two columns could balance by weight rather than by source order.
- **First-run card is marooned.** A 560 px card floating in the upper third of a 1280×900 field with the footer stranded below it. Centre it vertically, or let step 1 fill more of the frame — it is the only thing on screen and it looks like an accident.
- **Empty state points at Settings but doesn't go there.** "3 unpriced roles hidden — give them rates in Settings" is plain text, while the Margin block's identical instruction makes "Settings" a working link. Same sentence, two behaviours.
- **Breakdown sub-rows wrap mid-expression** at ≤861 px: "Production 40 h ×" / "1 050 kr". The multiplication reads as broken. `white-space: nowrap` on the sub-row label with the amount allowed to drop, or shorten to "40 h × 1 050".
- **Em-dash monoculture** (detector, advisory): 9 in body copy, doing every connective job. Vary two or three.

## Questions to Consider

- If the verdict is the product, what would this look like if you designed the panel *verdict-first* — the judgement at the top in display type, and the price as its supporting evidence?
- A "below target" state currently tells you that you are below. What would it take for it to tell you *what to change* — "raise the blended rate to 1 480 kr/h, or cut 22 hours"?
- The visual world is iOS on loan. Is there a version of this that looks like a Swedish consultancy's own instrument — something closer to a rate card or an invoice than to Settings.app — without giving up any of the clarity you have?
- Both copy outputs are described as first-class product surfaces, but neither can be previewed before it hits the clipboard. Should the client document be something you *see* rather than something you *paste and hope*?
